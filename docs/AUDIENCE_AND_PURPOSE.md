# Circle OS — Audience and Purpose

*Draft based on the founder’s account and product choices, November 8, 2026.*

This brief defines whom Circle aims to help, what they struggle with and what
the app should help them achieve. It separates the founder’s observations from
assumptions that need research. It describes intended value, not verified product
outcomes.

## Purpose

**Circle helps young unemployed graduates in Myanmar discover interests, choose
a direction and build trust in themselves through small actions and reflection.**

A person can begin without knowing their purpose, having a goal or feeling
motivated.

Circle helps them understand their starting point, choose something within reach
and recognize what their experience teaches them.

## Primary audience

The first audience is **unemployed graduates in their twenties in Myanmar who
feel stuck and unsure how to move forward**.

The founder particularly pictures people who:

- Graduated around 2020–2021 or earlier.
- Live with family, mostly in cities.
- Have limited money, opportunities or family support.
- May still be looking for work, or may have become discouraged and stopped.
- Have a basic understanding of English.
- Feel they have little to look forward to.

This is the founder’s starting picture, not a verified description of all
unemployed graduates.

**Secondary audience:** younger students facing similar uncertainty. People
unhappy in their jobs may also benefit, but the first experience should focus on
the primary audience.

## Their circumstances

The founder describes an environment where conflict, safety concerns, economic
hardship and limited work opportunities make independence difficult.

Studying or working abroad and starting a business can feel out of reach without
money or support. Access to international payment services and online income can
present further barriers. Limited English may also restrict access to information.

These are founder-reported circumstances. Specific economic, financial-service
and education claims require independent verification before being used as public
facts.

Circle must recognize that people face real external limits. Their circumstances
cannot be explained by mindset or discipline alone.

## The problem in their daily life

A person wakes up without a clear reason to engage with the day. They want life
to change but cannot see a realistic starting point.

Social media shows other people studying abroad, finding jobs or enjoying
opportunities they cannot access. Comparing lives can deepen the feeling of being
left behind.

Scrolling and other immediate pleasures can become an escape. Days pass with
little sense of deliberate choice or movement.

This pattern is a working hypothesis drawn from the founder’s experience and
observations. User research should test where it fits and where it does not.

## Pain points

- **Feeling trapped:** the routes they want appear blocked.
- **Lack of direction:** they do not know what interests them or what to pursue.
- **Comparison:** other people’s visible success makes their own efforts feel small.
- **Discouragement:** they struggle to believe that trying will make a difference.
- **Avoidance:** difficult questions are easier to postpone than approach.
- **Overlooked effort:** past attempts and achievements may no longer feel meaningful.

Fear of choosing wrongly may also matter, but its importance remains uncertain.

## What they want

The founder expects people might describe their needs as:

> “I want to find my purpose.”
> “I want a way out.”
> “I want something to look forward to.”

These are anticipated phrases, not interview quotations.

Their immediate hopes may include financial support, studying or working abroad,
or starting a business.

Circle’s intended contribution is to help them:

1. **Discover interests** through experience.
2. **Choose a direction** they want to explore.
3. **Build trust in themselves** through deliberate choices and follow-through.

They may also want to understand their feelings, care for themselves and feel
more positive about their own efforts.

## How Circle can help

| Need | Circle’s intended help |
| --- | --- |
| “I don’t know where to begin.” | Help them choose a small, manageable starting point. |
| “I don’t understand what I’m feeling.” | Give them a simple way to notice and express it. |
| “I don’t know what interests me.” | Support small experiments and reflection on what they discover. |
| “I never follow through.” | Help them practice keeping realistic commitments and understand interruptions. |
| “I haven’t achieved anything.” | Help them recognize real past and present effort they may overlook. |
| “Nothing is in my control.” | Help them identify choices and actions available within their circumstances. |

Possible activities include moving their body, reading, meditating, exploring an
interest or practicing a skill. These are examples, not a required routine for
everyone.

## Agency, discipline and rest

The founder believes the mind plays a central role in how a person meets life.

Circle should help people recognize thoughts and feelings, then practice choosing
their response. It should develop accountability, willpower and discipline through
actions the person chooses.

A thought or feeling does not have to decide the next action.

Rest is part of being human. A missed day does not erase earlier effort. Circle
should help someone distinguish needed rest from repeated avoidance without
assuming which one occurred.

## What progress means

Progress can include:

- Recognizing a feeling.
- Making a deliberate choice.
- Starting something.
- Following through on a chosen commitment.
- Doing something worthwhile that felt difficult.
- Discovering an interest or learning that something does not suit them.
- Returning after an interruption.

Finishing school or trying the gym for a month may be meaningful parts of
someone’s history, even if they currently feel stuck.

Circle should represent those experiences accurately. A stopped attempt remains
an attempt; it does not prove a permanent trait or current habit.

Recorded activity provides evidence of particular actions. It does not establish
a person’s total worth or an objective score for their willpower.

## Tone

Circle should be **clear, logical, respectful and grounded in the person’s own
evidence**.

It should acknowledge effort, describe patterns honestly and help the person
choose a next step. Encouragement should refer to something real.

For example, if their records support it:

> “You practiced twice earlier this week. Today doesn’t erase that. What made starting harder today?”

Circle should make room for both accountability and compassion.

## Boundaries

Circle’s responsibility is to support self-understanding, deliberate action and
recognition of progress.

It does not promise employment, income, migration opportunities, happiness or a
solution to external circumstances. It does not provide therapy or decide a
person’s purpose for them.

It should support realistic hope without guaranteeing a particular outcome.

## Language and trust

Simple English is the current starting direction. Burmese support is important
for reaching the wider intended audience; its delivery timing remains governed
by the existing product plan.

The founder expects users may accept AI-assisted reflection on their writing,
but this needs validation. The app should explain how writing is used and
discourage sensitive input. That explanation does not replace appropriate data
protection.

## Design ideas still being explored

These ideas are provisional:

- Short multiple-choice onboarding about the person’s current situation.
- A first home screen that acknowledges their starting point and past efforts.
- Progress drawn from their own experiences.
- More spacious daily tools.
- Ready-made Protocols with manageable, adjustable challenges.

The dashboard’s content and layout are undecided. No school-completion percentage
has been verified or adopted.

These ideas need separate design and implementation review. This brief does not
replace the existing App Box, brand or delivery rules.

## What we still need to learn

- How closely this audience description matches people’s actual experiences.
- What they have already tried and why it did not help enough.
- What would make them start, return to or stop using Circle.
- Which kinds of progress feel meaningful rather than patronizing.
- Whether onboarding questions feel understandable and relevant.
- How comfortable they are with private writing and AI.
- Whether simple English is sufficient for the first users.

Early success would mean people can identify something they learned about
themselves, a step they chose and evidence of effort they recognize. These are
proposed outcomes to test, not established results.
